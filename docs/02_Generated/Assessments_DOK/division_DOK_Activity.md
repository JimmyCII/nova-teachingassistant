Here is a DOK-leveled Group Activity for a 6th-grade Arizona math class based on standard 6.NS.B.2.

---

## **Arizona Math: The Great Division Challenge!**

**Standard:** 6.NS.B.2 - Fluently divide multi-digit numbers using the standard algorithm.
**Topic:** Division
**Grade Level:** 6th Grade
**Group Size:** 4 students per group
**Materials:** Whiteboards/large paper, markers, DOK activity sheets, calculators (for checking at the end, not for solving), scratch paper.

---

### **Group Roles:**

Each member of your group will have a specific role to ensure everyone participates and the task runs smoothly. Rotate roles for different activities if time permits, but for this session, choose one role for each DOK level's main activity.

*   **Facilitator:** Reads instructions aloud, ensures everyone understands the task, keeps the group focused and on time, encourages participation from all members.
*   **Recorder:** Writes down all calculations, answers, and group decisions clearly on the provided paper/whiteboard. Ensures work is legible and organized.
*   **Materials Manager:** Organizes all necessary supplies (markers, scratch paper, DOK activity sheets) for the group and ensures they are put away neatly after the activity.
*   **Presenter:** Clearly explains the group's solutions and reasoning for each DOK level to the class. Must be prepared to answer questions.

---

### **DOK 1 (Recall): "Division Quick Check"**

**Objective:** Students will recall basic division facts and identify the components of a division problem.

**Activity Description:**
As a warm-up, your group will quickly review basic division vocabulary and complete two fundamental division problems.

1.  **Vocabulary Match:** On your DOK 1 sheet, match the following terms with their definitions/examples:
    *   Dividend
    *   Divisor
    *   Quotient
    *   Remainder
2.  **Quick Computations:** Solve the following division problems without showing the full standard algorithm (mental math or quick recall is fine):
    *   $180 \div 15$
    *   $225 \div 9$

**Facilitator:** Ensure everyone agrees on the definitions and answers.
**Recorder:** Write down the matched definitions and solutions clearly.
**Materials Manager:** Distribute the DOK 1 sheet and markers.
**Presenter:** Be ready to share the group's definitions and answers.

---

### **DOK 2 (Skill/Concept): "Collaborative Division Challenge"**

**Objective:** Students will fluently divide multi-digit numbers using the standard algorithm, showing all steps.

**Activity Description:**
Your group will work together to solve a set of multi-digit division problems. For each problem, you *must* show all steps of the standard algorithm clearly.

1.  **Problem 1:** $3,456 \div 16$
2.  **Problem 2:** $8,290 \div 35$
3.  **Problem 3 (Word Problem):** A large Arizona farm harvested 12,740 pounds of pecans. They want to package them into bags that each hold 28 pounds. How many full bags of pecans can they make? How many pounds of pecans will be left over?

**Facilitator:** Guide the group through each step of the division algorithm, making sure everyone understands and agrees on each part.
**Recorder:** Accurately write down the standard algorithm steps for each problem on the whiteboard or large paper. Clearly state the final answers, including the remainder for Problem 3.
**Materials Manager:** Ensure enough space and markers are available for showing detailed work.
**Presenter:** Be ready to explain the steps your group took to solve each problem, especially the word problem, to the class.

### **DOK 3 (Strategic Thinking): "Arizona Water Distribution Dilemma"**

**Objective:** Students will apply multi-digit division in a complex, multi-step problem, interpret remainders, and justify their decisions and solutions.

**Activity Description:**
Imagine your group is part of the Arizona Department of Water Resources. You have been allocated a total of **85,500 gallons of water** to distribute among three small Arizona communities for drought relief. The communities have different needs based on their populations:

*   **Community A (Desert Bloom):** Population 1,250
*   **Community B (Canyon Ridge):** Population 980
*   **Community C (Riverbend Crossing):** Population 1,570

**Your Task:**

1.  **Calculate Fair Share:** Determine how many gallons of water *each person* would receive if the water was divided *equally among all people* in all three communities combined. (Round down to the nearest whole gallon, as you can't give partial gallons easily).
2.  **Community-Specific Distribution:** Now, calculate how many gallons each community would receive if the 85,500 gallons were distributed proportionally based on their *individual community populations*. (For example, if Community A has 10% of the total population, it gets 10% of the water). Round down to the nearest whole gallon for each community.
3.  **The Remainder Problem:** After distributing proportionally (from step 2), will there be any water left over? If so, how much?
4.  **Decision & Justification:** Your group must decide:
    *   Which distribution method (equal per person, or proportional by community) do you think is fairer or more practical in this situation? Why?
    *   What would you do with any leftover water from your chosen method (from step 3)? Justify your decision.

**Facilitator:** Guide the discussion, ensuring all group members contribute ideas for distribution methods and the justification for the leftover water.
**Recorder:** Document all calculations for both distribution methods. Clearly write down the group's chosen method, the amount of leftover water, and the group's plan for it, along with the justifications.
**Materials Manager:** Ensure the DOK 3 sheet is available and manage any scratch paper needed for complex calculations.
**Presenter:** Be prepared to present your group's calculations, chosen distribution method, and detailed justification for how you handled the leftover water. Explain the pros and cons of each method considered.
