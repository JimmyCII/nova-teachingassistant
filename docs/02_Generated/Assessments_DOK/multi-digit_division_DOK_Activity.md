## DOK-Leveled Group Activity: Multi-Digit Division Mastery

**Standard:** 6.NS.B.2 - Fluently divide multi-digit numbers using the standard algorithm.
**Topic:** Multi-Digit Division
**Grade Level:** 6th Grade
**Group Size:** 4 Students
**Materials:** Whiteboards/large paper, markers, worksheets, scratch paper.

---

### Group Roles:

*   **Facilitator:** Reads all instructions aloud, ensures everyone understands the task, keeps the group on track and participating, and encourages discussion.
*   **Recorder:** Writes down all group answers, calculations, and explanations clearly on the provided materials.
*   **Presenter:** Prepares to share the group's solutions and reasoning for DOK 2 and DOK 3 with the class.
*   **Resource Manager/Timekeeper:** Manages all group materials (worksheets, markers, scratch paper), monitors the time for each activity section, and ensures materials are organized.

---

### 1. DOK 1 (Recall): "Division Vocabulary Check-Up"

**Objective:** Students will recall basic division facts and identify the parts of a division problem.
**Time Allotment:** 5 minutes

**Activity:**
Each group will receive a small whiteboard or worksheet section.

1.  **Facilitator:** Read the following problems and definitions aloud.
2.  **Everyone:** Collaborate to answer the questions.
3.  **Recorder:** Write down the group's answers.

**Questions:**

*   **Problem 1:** In the equation $36 \div 4 = 9$:
    *   What is the dividend?
    *   What is the divisor?
    *   What is the quotient?
*   **Problem 2:** Solve these simple division facts mentally:
    *   $56 \div 7 = ?$
    *   $81 \div 9 = ?$
*   **Problem 3:** What does "remainder" mean in the context of division? Briefly explain in your own words.

---

### 2. DOK 2 (Skill/Concept): "Division Challenge Grid"

**Objective:** Students will collaboratively solve multi-digit division problems using the standard algorithm.
**Time Allotment:** 15-20 minutes

**Activity:**
Your group will receive a worksheet with three multi-digit division problems. Work together to solve each problem, showing all steps of the standard algorithm clearly.

1.  **Facilitator:** Read each division problem aloud to ensure everyone understands the numbers involved.
2.  **Everyone:** Work together to solve each problem using the standard algorithm. Discuss each step (divide, multiply, subtract, bring down) to ensure accuracy. You may choose to have one person lead the calculation for one problem while others check, then switch roles.
3.  **Recorder:** Clearly write down all the steps of the standard algorithm for each problem on the worksheet.
4.  **Presenter:** Be prepared to explain the steps for *one* of the problems to the class, demonstrating the standard algorithm.

**Problems:**

*   **Problem A:** $3,456 \div 8$
*   **Problem B:** $7,125 \div 15$
*   **Problem C:** $12,804 \div 28$

---

### 3. DOK 3 (Strategic Thinking): "The School Field Trip Budget"

**Objective:** Students will apply multi-digit division to solve a real-world problem, interpret remainders, and justify their reasoning.
**Time Allotment:** 20-25 minutes

**Activity:**
The 6th-grade class is planning a field trip to the Arizona Science Center. They have a total budget of **$3,750** for transportation. Each bus rental costs **$275**.

1.  **Facilitator:** Read the scenario and the questions aloud to the group.
2.  **Everyone:** Work together to solve the problem. This requires planning how to use division, performing the calculation, and carefully interpreting any remainders. Discuss your reasoning and come to a consensus.
3.  **Recorder:** Document all calculations, the group's final answers, and a clear explanation for each answer on your worksheet.
4.  **Presenter:** Be ready to explain your group's solution, including how you used division, what your remainder meant, and why you made the decisions you did.

**Questions:**

*   **Part 1:** How many full buses can the school afford to rent with the $3,750 budget, if each bus costs $275?
*   **Part 2:** After renting the maximum number of full buses, how much money will be left over from the transportation budget?
*   **Part 3:** Each bus can hold 48 students. If the school rents the maximum number of full buses you calculated in Part 1, how many total students can go on the field trip?
*   **Part 4:** If there are 150 students in the 6th grade, will *all* students be able to go on the field trip based on your calculations? Explain your reasoning.
