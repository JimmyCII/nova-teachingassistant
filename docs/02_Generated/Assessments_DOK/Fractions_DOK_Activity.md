## DOK-Leveled Group Activity: Dividing Fractions - Community Garden Design

**Standard:** 6.NS.A.1 - Interpret and compute quotients of fractions, and solve word problems involving division of fractions by fractions, e.g., by using visual fraction models and equations to represent the problem.

**Topic:** Dividing Fractions
**Grade Level:** 6th Grade
**Subject:** Math

---

### Group Roles:

To ensure active participation and diverse contributions, each group member will have a specific role:

*   **Facilitator:** Guides discussion, keeps the group on task and time, ensures all voices are heard, and helps resolve minor disagreements.
*   **Recorder:** Neatly writes down the group's work, solutions, and justifications on provided paper/whiteboard.
*   **Visualizer/Materials Manager:** Responsible for accurately creating visual fraction models (using drawings, fraction bars, or other manipulatives), manages group materials, and ensures tools are used correctly.
*   **Presenter:** Prepares and clearly communicates the group's final solutions and reasoning to the class.

---

### 1. DOK 1 (Recall): Fraction Fact Finders (Warm-Up)

*   **Objective:** Students will recall and interpret the meaning of dividing a whole number by a unit fraction using a visual model.
*   **Scenario:** You are hosting a small party! You have 3 whole pizzas, and each person wants a serving that is 1/4 of a pizza.
*   **Activity:**
    1.  **Individually:** Each group member should quickly draw a visual representation (e.g., circles or rectangles) for the 3 pizzas.
    2.  **Visualizer:** Now, work to divide the visual model to show servings of 1/4 of a pizza from each whole.
    3.  **Facilitator:** Guide a brief group discussion to confirm everyone understands what they are looking for.
    4.  **Recorder:** Write down the group's confirmed answer to the question: **"How many 1/4 pizza servings can you get from 3 whole pizzas?"**
*   **Expected Outcome:** Students should visually demonstrate $3 \div \frac{1}{4}$ and collectively determine there are 12 servings.

---

### 2. DOK 2 (Skill/Concept): Recipe Remix

*   **Objective:** Students will interpret and compute quotients of fractions, solving a word problem using both a visual model and an equation.
*   **Scenario:** A local bakery is making a special batch of mini-muffins. The recipe calls for $\frac{5}{6}$ of a cup of flour for the entire batch. If each mini-muffin requires $\frac{1}{12}$ of a cup of flour, how many mini-muffins can the baker make?
*   **Activity:**
    1.  **Visualizer:** Begin by accurately drawing a visual fraction model (e.g., a fraction bar or number line) representing the $\frac{5}{6}$ cup of flour.
    2.  **Group Collaboration:** Work together to divide the $\frac{5}{6}$ model into sections that are $\frac{1}{12}$ of a cup each. Clearly label your divisions.
    3.  **Facilitator:** Guide the group to set up the corresponding division equation that represents this problem.
    4.  **Recorder:** Write down the equation and compute the final numerical answer.
    5.  **Presenter:** Be ready to explain how your visual model and equation both support your answer.
*   **Expected Outcome:** Students will draw a model of $\frac{5}{6}$ divided into $\frac{1}{12}$ sections, set up the equation $\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{1}{12}$, compute 10, and state that the baker can make 10 mini-muffins.

### 3. DOK 3 (Strategic Thinking): Community Garden Design Challenge

*   **Objective:** Students will apply fractional division in a multi-step, open-ended problem, interpret remainders, justify their reasoning, and propose practical solutions.
*   **Scenario:** Your community has received a generous donation of a rectangular plot of land that is $\frac{3}{4}$ of an acre. The community wants to divide this land into smaller, equally sized garden plots for families. Each family garden plot needs to be $\frac{1}{8}$ of an acre.
*   **Activity:**
    1.  **Facilitator:** Ensure the group understands the entire challenge and plans an organized approach to solve all parts of the problem.
    2.  **Visualizer:** Create a detailed and accurate visual model (e.g., an area model or fraction diagram) to represent the $\frac{3}{4}$ acre and how it is divided into $\frac{1}{8}$ acre plots.
    3.  **Recorder:** Write down the division equation needed to solve the problem and compute the numerical answer.
    4.  **As a Group, Discuss and Answer the Following Questions in Detail:**
        *   **A.** How many *full* family garden plots can be created from the $\frac{3}{4}$ acre plot?
        *   **B.** What fraction of an acre will be leftover after creating the full plots? Show your work for this.
        *   **C.** Brainstorm and justify at least two *different* practical uses for the leftover land. Why would these uses be good for the community?
        *   **D.** Explain how your visual model and equation both clearly support your answers for A and B.
    5.  **Presenter:** Prepare to clearly share your group's model, equation, all answers (A-D), and the justifications for your proposals with the class.
*   **Expected Outcome:**
    *   Students compute $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{1}{8} = 6$.
    *   They correctly identify that 6 full family garden plots can be created.
    *   They determine there is no leftover land (remainder is 0).
    *   They propose practical uses for the plots (e.g., 6 families, no waste).
    *   They articulate how their visual model and equation confirm their solution.

**Revised DOK 3 (Strategic Thinking): Community Garden Design Challenge**

*   **Objective:** Students will apply fractional division in a multi-step, open-ended problem, interpret remainders, justify their reasoning, and propose practical solutions.
*   **Scenario:** Your community has received a generous donation of a rectangular plot of land that is $\frac{5}{6}$ of an acre. The community wants to divide this land into smaller, equally sized garden plots for families. Each family garden plot needs to be $\frac{1}{4}$ of an acre.
*   **Activity:**
    1.  **Facilitator:** Ensure the group understands the entire challenge and plans an organized approach to solve all parts of the problem.
    2.  **Visualizer:** Create a detailed and accurate visual model (e.g., an area model or fraction diagram) to represent the $\frac{5}{6}$ acre and how it is divided into $\frac{1}{4}$ acre plots.
    3.  **Recorder:** Write down the division equation needed to solve the problem and compute the numerical answer.
    4.  **As a Group, Discuss and Answer the Following Questions in Detail:**
        *   **A.** How many *full* family garden plots can be created from the $\frac{5}{6}$ acre plot?
        *   **B.** What fraction of an acre will be leftover after creating the full plots? Show your work for this. (Hint: Think about what part of a $\frac{1}{4}$ acre plot is left, then convert that back to a fraction of an acre.)
        *   **C.** Brainstorm and justify at least two *different* practical uses for the leftover land. Why would these uses be good for the community?
        *   **D.** Explain how your visual model and equation both clearly support your answers for A and B.
    5.  **Presenter:** Prepare to clearly share your group's model, equation, all answers (A-D), and the justifications for your proposals with the class.
*   **Expected Outcome:**
    *   Students compute $\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{1}{4} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{4}{1} = \frac{20}{6} = \frac{10}{3} = 3 \frac{1}{3}$.
    *   They correctly identify that 3 full family garden plots can be created.
    *   They determine that $\frac{1}{3}$ of a $\frac{1}{4}$ acre plot is leftover, which means $\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{12}$ of an acre is leftover.
    *   They propose practical uses for $\frac{1}{12}$ acre (e.g., a small herb garden, a seating area, a compost bin, a small flower bed).
    *   They articulate how their visual model and equation confirm their solution, especially the remainder.
